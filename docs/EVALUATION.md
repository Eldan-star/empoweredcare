# Evaluation: what M3 can and cannot do yet

_October 2026. The full numbers are in `evaluation/reports/2026-10-10-m3-baseline.md`
(and `.json`). The method is in METHODOLOGY §5._

## What was tested

There is no Ethiopian DHIS2 history yet, so everything was tested on **simulated** measles.
We built a stand-in Ethiopia:
- **Places:** 400 woreda-sized units, with a median of about 108,000 people, clustered into
  12 regions, with first-dose coverage varying by region.
- **Outbreaks:** generated by IDM's laser-measles for 15 years. The first 4 years were
  thrown away as warm-up.
- **Reporting:** passed through a layer that behaves like PHEM reporting:
  - each woreda reports a different share of true infections (about 12% on average);
  - reports arrive 0–4 weeks late;
  - about 10% of weeks are never reported;
  - about 15% arrive in a late batch.

The system only ever sees what had arrived by each week, as it would in real life.

Five versions of the simulated world were run:

| Scenario | What changes |
|---|---|
| gravity | Disease spreads between places by the gravity pattern, the same idea our model uses |
| radiation, stouffer | Disease spreads by **different** patterns from our model's. This tests that our model doesn't only work when its assumptions are right |
| low-reporting | Only about 5% of infections are reported |
| episodic | Lower transmission, so places are quiet between outbreaks more often |

## Results in plain language

### 1. EPHI's thresholds (detection)

| | Catches outbreaks | When an alarm fires, real transmission is under way | Typical delay |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alert threshold** (1 suspected case) | 73–77% | 80–84% | about 1 week after onset |
| **Action threshold** (5 suspected or 3 confirmed in 4 weeks) | 33–36% of all outbreaks, about 46% of large ones, within 8 weeks of onset | about 92% | 3–4 weeks after onset |

**What this means:**
- The **single-case alert is the early warning.** It fires within about a week and is
  usually real.
- The **action threshold is reliable but late.** Many outbreaks only reach it more than 8
  weeks after they start. This matches EPHI's design (alert = investigate, action =
  respond), and it shows why acting on alerts quickly matters.
- **With low reporting, everything gets worse.** When only about 5% of infections are
  reported, the action threshold catches about 10% of outbreaks in time. In woredas that
  report poorly, the thresholds cannot be relied on, which is why "surveillance silent" is
  flagged and never shown as "no cases".

### 2. The risk index: where measles will be heaviest next quarter ✅

Each month we took the top 10% of woredas by each method and checked how many were really
among the 10% with the most measles over the next 3 months. Picking at random gives 10%.

| Method | Correct |
|---|---|
| **Our risk index** | **44–53%** |
| Lowest vaccination coverage | 28–31% |
| Most cases in the last 12 months | 24–29% |
| Random | 10% |

The index wins clearly in every scenario, including the ones where the disease spreads
differently from our model's assumptions. **This use is ready to show**, labelled as a
ranking for "where measles will be heaviest next quarter".

### 3. The risk index: which quiet woreda will ignite next ❌ (not yet)

Among woredas reporting no cases, we checked whether the index picks out the ones where a
new outbreak starts next quarter.

| Method | Correct |
|---|---|
| Our risk index | 6–21% |
| Lowest vaccination coverage | 9–22% |
| Random | 9–21% |
| Reference: the simulator's true susceptible children | 14–31% |

**The index does no better than random here.** By our own rule (METHODOLOGY 4.6), it must
not be presented as predicting where outbreaks will ignite.

The reason is clear from the reconstruction results:
- **The reporting rate is estimated well.** The error is about 0.015 against a true 0.11,
  and the ranking matches the truth well (rank correlation 0.77–0.95).
- **Rebuilding the count of susceptible children from case data does not work well.** Its
  ranking agrees only weakly with the truth (rank correlation about 0.2–0.35). It is a
  small number left over after subtracting large, noisy numbers, which is a known
  weakness of this method.

Even perfect knowledge of susceptible children only reaches 14–31%. Ignition is partly
luck: it depends on when an infected person happens to arrive.

## What this changes

1. **Show the index for its proven use only:** "where measles will be heaviest next
   quarter". Keep the ignition use internal.
2. **Make the susceptible-children estimate more robust** (next modelling step).
   Candidate fixes, to be tested with this same harness:
   - a Bayesian or state-space version;
   - anchoring to coverage surveys;
   - capping how far case data can pull it.
3. **Validate on real data as soon as possible**, starting with past EPHI bulletins.
   Synthetic results show the methods behave sensibly; they cannot prove the model works
   in Ethiopia.
4. **Lead with the alert threshold for timeliness.** Rank alerts using the risk index and
   early-rise flags.

## How to run it yourself

laser-measles needs a separate Python environment, because its numerical libraries
conflict with the web app's. In PowerShell, from the project folder:

```powershell
py -3.12 -m venv .venv-sim
.venv-sim\Scripts\Activate.ps1
pip install -r requirements-sim.txt
pip install sqlalchemy psycopg[binary] python-dotenv   # only for --units db
python -m evaluation.harness --quick     # about 10 seconds, a small check
python -m evaluation.harness             # about 1 minute, writes evaluation\reports\
```

- `--units db` uses the real OCHA woredas from your database, with real populations and
  centroids, instead of synthetic places. Vaccination coverage is synthetic unless you have
  loaded it.
- Simulated outbreaks are still not real data, whichever places you use.
